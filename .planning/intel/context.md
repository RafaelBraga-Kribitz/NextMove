## Topic: Open decisions awaiting ADR ratification
- source: OPEN_DECISIONS.md
- The document states these decisions "must be ratified (as ADRs) before `/gsd:plan-phase`" and that recommendations are defaults which contrary evidence during planning may overturn, with an ADR either way. Its closing planning instruction states OD-1…OD-10 should be ratified as ADR-001…ADR-010, and any later change requires a superseding ADR. As of this ingest, no `docs/adr/NNN-*.md` files are present in the ingest set.

- **OD-1 — Modular monolith or services?** Options: (A) modular monolith, one container; (B) 2–3 services (decision API, pipeline worker, dashboard); (C) full microservices. Trade-offs: A fastest and honest at this scale, single failure domain, risks blurred boundaries without discipline; B demonstrates service thinking but doubles ops surface; C pure cost, violates production-realism principle. **Recommendation: A**, with registry interfaces designed service-ready and boundary discipline enforced by import-linting between packages.
- **OD-2 — Decide-on-request or decide-in-batch?** Options: (A) batch decisions materialized nightly, API reads them; (B) on-demand computation per request; (C) hybrid — batch for populations, on-demand for single-customer queries. Trade-offs: A matches the corpus's offline-heavy consensus and marketing cadence but makes the demo feel static; B is demo-friendly and simpler per-request but recomputes constantly; C costs a little duplication. **Recommendation: C.** Batch is the operational truth (CRM feed export); the same engine invoked synchronously powers the interactive demo. One engine, two invocation modes.
- **OD-3 — Simulator boundary.** Options: (A) same repo, isolated package with lint-enforced import ban toward `models/`; (B) separate repository. Contamination risk noted: if simulator internals are importable by models, results are fake. **Recommendation: A** with an import-linter contract and a CI check; the isolation guarantee is itself a portfolio artifact.
- **OD-4 — Synthetic-only or synthetic + public-dataset validation?** Options: (A) simulator only; (B) simulator plus a public clickstream run through ingestion/features/segmentation to prove pipeline generality; (C) chase a real partner dataset. **Recommendation: B.** Decisions evaluated in simulation (labeled as such); data engineering validated on real-shaped public data.
- **OD-5 — Is a non-attributable model ever admissible?** Options: (A) hard rule, decision path requires attributions; (B) allow black-box if it wins by a large margin, with surrogate explanations. **Recommendation: A.** Black-box challengers may appear in the comparison study only; the finding "we declined X% accuracy for faithful explanations" is itself a strong result.
- **OD-6 — Uplift approximation in MVP.** Options: (A) approximate uplift as P(convert|action) − P(convert|none) from response models, quantify approximation error against simulator ground truth, uplift learners as stretch; (B) T-/X-learners in MVP. **Recommendation: A**, with the approximation-error analysis promoted to a headline evaluation section.
- **OD-7 — Artifact versioning: DVC or content-hashed store + MLflow artifacts?** Trade-offs: DVC standard and recognizable with some workflow friction; hash-store lighter, custom, less legible to reviewers. **Recommendation: DVC** for data/artifacts plus MLflow for runs/models — recognizability wins for a portfolio. (ARCHITECTURAL_DIRECTION.md §8, the locked ADR, leaves this explicitly `[Open: OD-7]`.)
- **OD-8 — 3WD thresholds: static or dynamic?** Trade-offs: static (fixed MDE plus ±2%-class indifference margin) is corpus-sanctioned and shippable; dynamic (traffic/seasonality-aware) is the flagged research novelty but multiplies tuning surface. **Recommendation:** static in MVP with the threshold interface designed for dynamism; dynamic thresholds as the first post-MVP experiment.
- **OD-9 — Automation vs. human control.** **Recommendation:** tiered autonomy — content/ranking micro-changes auto-apply on 3WD Accept; policy/model promotions and segment redefinitions always require human sign-off; anything touching price/discount ceilings is human-only. Tiers live in config.
- **OD-10 — Action space flexibility vs. simplicity.** A huge parameterized action space makes ranking and evaluation intractable; a tiny one makes the demo trivial. **Recommendation:** MVP fixes ~8 action archetypes (none, wait, recommend, bundle, discount-tier×2, email-now, email-delayed) with small parameter grids; the ActionProvider registry is the escape hatch. Revisit only after policy evaluation works.

## Topic: Distilled research principles
- source: RESEARCH_SYNTHESIS.md (§1)
- 1. **Optimize decisions, not predictions** — the contract is a decision object; models are internal components scored by decision value, not leaderboard metrics.
- 2. **Business constraints belong inside model selection, not after it** — context-free metrics (Silhouette, Davies-Bouldin) produce economically unviable segments; the MCDA wrapper (TOPSIS/PROMETHEE II class) with min-cluster-size, max-clusters, resource-cost criteria is a core module, not an add-on.
- 3. **Semi-adaptive beats both extremes** — fully adaptive UIs disorient and destroy trust; fully adaptable UIs are ignored (paradox of the active user). System proposes, human can override; overrides captured as high-weight feedback. No silent, irreversible adaptation.
- 4. **Binary A/B testing is insufficient for multi-metric commerce decisions** — adjudication must support Accept / Reject / Delay with MDE thresholds and an indifference margin ("dead zone").
- 5. **Split heavy offline computation from light online serving** — batch-first architecture; the online API reads precomputed decisions/features; no real-time training in MVP.
- 6. **Short-term engagement metrics are adversarial** — reward/utility functions must include margin, incentive cost, and fatigue/trust penalties; report longitudinal proxies, not just CR.
- 7. **Explanation is a trust-load-bearing feature, not documentation** — explanations maximize relevance framing and avoid creepy specificity; user/manager-facing data controls.
- 8. **Hybridize** — representation learning for slow preferences, bandits for fast context, rules for guardrails; layered policy where batch models produce priors, a bandit (stretch) adapts within-session/period, and deterministic rules always constrain both.
- 9. **Micro-conversions (PCR) are the early-warning channel** — instrument scroll/filter/dwell-style micro-events in the simulator; use empirically derived (not expert-guessed) micro-conversion weights, a flagged research gap.
- 10. **Cold start needs an explicit, boring answer** — deterministic fallback routes unknowns to the best generalized variant, labeled as cold-start in the reasoning.
- 11. **Generative content requires a validation firewall** — GenUI only as an optional plugin: pre-generated offline, validated against catalog ground truth, served statically. Never in the core decision path.
- 12. **Evaluate on three axes or not at all** — prediction quality, decision quality (uplift/regret/constrained profit), system quality (latency, reproducibility, explainability).

## Topic: Consensus findings across research strands
- source: RESEARCH_SYNTHESIS.md (§2)
- 1. **The binary A/B failure mode** — bundled changes obscure causality; marginal results force premature decisions; multi-metric conflicts (CR ↑ / AOV ↓) are common. The corpus proposes formal Delay states and indifference margins.
- 2. **The black-box trust deficit** — fully autonomous adaptation is empirically rejected by users across TAM studies, well-being studies, and MSME reviews. Transparency plus override controls are convergent requirements, and double as clean feedback data.
- 3. **Short-term metric misalignment** — click-maximizing systems reward-hack; longitudinal tracking (retention, LTV, fatigue) is the repeatedly demanded fix.
- 4. **Layered hybrid architectures win** — batch representation/segmentation plus online lightweight adaptation plus rule guardrails appears in every applied system in the corpus.
- 5. **Personalization value is real and large when segment-conditioned** — the anchor paper's field results (+27.6% CR, +68.1% AOV in specific clusters) establish the ceiling; effects are heterogeneous across clusters, which is itself an argument for decision-level (not global) optimization.

## Topic: Resolved contradictions and trade-offs
- source: RESEARCH_SYNTHESIS.md (§3)
- **T1 — Adaptive vs. adaptable interface control.** Resolution: semi-adaptive. System proposes; user/manager overrides; overrides feed back as labeled signals.
- **T2 — Single-metric vs. multi-criteria model selection.** Resolution: MCDA for offline macro-decisions (segmentation, algorithm selection) with weights exposed in config and sensitivity-checked; plain metrics acceptable for lightweight micro-optimizations. Weight-subjectivity risk mitigated by documenting weights as ADR-tracked config, not code.
- **T3 — Binary A/B vs. three-way decision.** Resolution: 3WD as the default gateway for policy/variant changes, with (a) a static indifference margin (±2% class) to prevent noise-chasing and (b) a maximum-delay budget that escalates to human review.
- **T4 — Prediction-first vs. decision-first optimization.** Resolution: decision-first as the product contract; prediction metrics retained as internal diagnostics. Uplift estimation enters as a stretch precisely because the corpus warns against fake counterfactual certainty.
- **T5 — Real-time vs. batch personalization.** Resolution: batch/near-line decisions in MVP; real-time adaptation only inside the simulator (bandit stretch goal), where its dynamics can be studied honestly without infra theater.
- **T6 — Bandit exploration vs. experience protection.** Noted as unresolved in the literature. Resolution: guardrailed exploration — rules bound the action set the bandit may explore; reward includes fatigue/margin penalties; regret and reward reported with smoothing.
- **T7 — GenUI ambition vs. hallucination liability.** Resolution: excluded from core; optional plugin restricted to offline pre-generation plus deterministic catalog validation plus static serving.

## Topic: Research gaps worth exploiting
- source: RESEARCH_SYNTHESIS.md (§4)
- Ranked by frequency in corpus × single-developer feasibility × portfolio value: (1) an open, automated experimentation gateway with 3WD routing and indifference margins — named a "massive gap in the current MLOps ecosystem" and identified as *the project's flagship differentiator*; (2) business-constrained evaluation wrappers for standard algorithms (scikit-learn-style API ranking clustering/model candidates by MCDA including viability and cost criteria); (3) data-driven micro-conversion weights (the corpus admits PCR weights are currently expert-guessed); (4) decision-level explanation composition (constraints fired + model attribution + counterfactual "why not the runner-up action"); (5) restraint as a first-class action — no system in the corpus models do nothing / wait / suppress discount explicitly; (6) longitudinal fatigue/LTV proxies in simulation.
- Gaps deliberately *not* pursued (documented so reviewers see the judgment): federated learning (infeasible solo), autonomous UI-generating RL (corpus rates feasibility low), preference-trajectory modeling (novel but orthogonal to the decision thesis).

## Topic: Design principles
- source: DESIGN_PRINCIPLES.md
- 1. **Opinionated over generic** — NextMove solves e-commerce behavioral decisioning, not "any ML decision problem." The action catalog, utility function, and constraint vocabulary are e-commerce-native (discount, bundle, timing, channel, inventory, margin). Extensibility exists, but the defaults are the product; feature requests that generalize the domain are rejected by policy.
- 2. **Headless architecture** — the intelligence layer is fully separated from any presentation; the contract is the typed API/JSON. The dashboard is a disposable demo client with zero privileged access; anything the dashboard shows must be obtainable from the public API; adapters (in/out) are the growth path.
- 3. **Event-driven** — the system's memory is an append-only event log; state (features, segments, decisions, outcomes) is derived, and delivered actions/overrides are themselves events. No component mutates history; "what did the system know at time t" is always answerable; the simulator and real adapters produce the identical event shape. Events help specifically at feedback capture, experiment attribution, and reproducible replay; where they do not help (synchronous single-decision requests), they are not forced.
- 4. **Explainable by default** — every important output (decision, segment, experiment verdict) ships with reasoning produced by the same pipeline, not written after the fact. The `Decision` schema makes `reasoning` and `reasoning_trace` required; models without usable attributions are inadmissible in the decision path; an explanation style guide enforces relevance-framing; explanation quality has its own review rubric.
- 5. **Human-in-the-loop** — the system proposes; humans can approve, override, and bound it. Semi-adaptive, never autonomous. Override endpoint plus `needs_human_review` queue in MVP; 3WD Delay escalates to humans after its budget; constraint config is the human steering wheel; overrides are logged as high-weight labeled events and reported in evaluation.
- 6. **Offline-first experimentation** — every policy, model, and variant is evaluated in reproducible offline simulation/replay before any (simulated) rollout; local development runs the entire world on a laptop. The simulator is a first-class, tested component; `make reproduce` is the primary developer loop; nothing requires cloud resources; online-style evaluation (3WD gating) runs inside the simulation clock.
- 7. **Reproducibility** — deterministic pipelines, versioned artifacts, documented experiments; identical inputs ⇒ identical outputs. Seeds everywhere (including exploration); content-hashed lineage on derived tables; MLflow run IDs cited in reports; golden-file tests on decision objects; CI runs a miniature end-to-end reproduction.
- 8. **Production realism** — the system resembles something a mid-market company could actually operate: one container, boring storage, declarative config, tests, runbooks, and honest omissions. No distributed systems without a load-bearing reason; every shortcut is documented as debt; failure modes from the corpus (delay loops, reward hacking, segment collapse) have explicit fail-safes and tests.

## Topic: Product identity and pitch
- source: PROJECT_IDENTITY.md
- **Elevator pitch:** NextMove is an opinionated, headless behavioral decision engine for e-commerce that turns raw customer events and business context into explainable next-best-actions — including the action of doing nothing. Instead of predicting clicks, it optimizes decisions: which intervention, for whom, when, through which channel, under real constraints like inventory, margin, and message fatigue. Every decision ships with expected impact, confidence, and a reason a marketing manager can read — and every change to the system must survive a three-way (accept/reject/delay) experimentation gateway before it ships.
- **Value proposition (one sentence):** NextMove tells an e-commerce team what to do next for each customer — and proves, before rollout, that the advice is worth taking.

## Topic: Differentiation from adjacent tool categories
- source: PROJECT_IDENTITY.md
- **vs. recommendation engines:** recommenders answer "which item?" over a single action type, optimized for engagement, blind to margin, inventory, incentive cost, and timing, and incapable of recommending restraint. NextMove treats "recommend a product" as one candidate in an action space including discounts, bundles, timing, channel choice, waiting, and doing nothing — ranked by expected incremental constrained profit. A recommender can be plugged into NextMove as one `ActionProvider`; the reverse is impossible.
- **vs. CDPs:** a CDP unifies identities and audiences and ends where judgment begins. NextMove assumes the data problem is roughly solved and owns the layer CDPs delegate: constrained decision-making with quantified expected outcomes and built-in experimental verification. No identity resolution, no connector marketplace, no audience UI.
- **vs. marketing automation:** automation executes hand-written if-then journeys with static rules, no learning, no uplift estimation, and open/click dashboards. NextMove inverts each property: rules are guardrails around learned policies; targeting aims at incremental effect; timing and channel are decision variables rather than journey constants; every policy change is adjudicated by a statistical gateway allowed to say "we don't know yet." Automation tools are downstream executors, not competitors.
- **One-line contrast:** Recommender — "People like you bought this." CDP — "Here is everything we know about them." Marketing automation — "On day 3, send email B." NextMove — "Don't discount this customer — show the premium bundle in 6 hours by email; expected uplift +3.8%, confidence 82%, and here's why."

## Topic: Why the project exists
- source: PROJECT_VISION.md (§2)
- Digital commerce platforms overwhelmingly serve static, one-size-fits-all experiences and campaign logic that ignore heterogeneous needs, cognitive limits, and shifting intent; documented consequences are cognitive overload, decision paralysis, and cart abandonment. Where personalization exists it is typically a narrow recommender disconnected from business reality, optimizing prediction rather than decisions. The corpus repeatedly shows mathematically "optimal" ML outputs are commercially unviable: clustering producing segments too small to serve, bandits reward-hacking short-term clicks while destroying lifetime value, and binary A/B tests forcing premature ship/kill calls. A third failure mode is trust destruction — deep, opaque personalization triggers the personalization–privacy paradox where relevance is welcomed but perceived surveillance causes psychological reactance and churn.
- **Why current solutions are insufficient:** recommendation engines have the wrong action space; marketing automation / journey builders have no learning, no uplift estimation, no principled evaluation; CDPs unify data but delegate decisions; academic adaptive-UI research has produced strong components (MCDA-constrained segmentation, three-way decision experimentation, semi-adaptive control, TWN+bandit nudging) but no one has assembled them into a coherent, explainable decision system — the synthesis explicitly identifies this integration gap as the opportunity.
- **Why now:** two forces converged in the 2024–2026 literature — the failure of pure-ML personalization is now empirically documented, and the required components (gradient boosting, bandits, SHAP, MCDA, sequential testing) are individually mature and cheap to operate. Assumption: mid-market e-commerce cannot afford Amazon-scale infrastructure but can afford an opinionated decision layer on top of data it already collects.
- **Context:** Market — the buyer-shaped hole is between "spreadsheet + gut feel" and "enterprise personalization suite" (assumption). Technical — the corpus converges on an offline-heavy / online-light split, meaning production realism is achievable by a single engineer without distributed systems. Business — the anchor paper demonstrates the value ceiling: on a real platform (438,261 sessions for segmentation, 99,193 for testing), cluster-tailored UI changes produced +27.56% CR and +68.09% AOV in one cluster.

## Topic: Portfolio positioning
- source: PROJECT_VISION.md (§3)
- The project answers the hiring manager's question ("Could I put this person on our personalization/CRM/growth stack next Monday?") by demonstrating Marketing Data Science (CLV, propensity, uplift framing, discount economics, channel/timing decisions), Decision Intelligence (explicit action spaces, constraint filtering, expected-value ranking, regret, three-way experimentation), Machine Learning (honest baseline → GBM → optional neural comparison, calibration, off-policy evaluation), Data & Software Engineering (event schemas, feature pipelines, typed decision API, adapters, tests), MLOps (experiment tracking, versioned artifacts, reproducible runs, model cards), and Product thinking (personas, manager-readable explanations, human-in-the-loop overrides).
- The ML is deliberately one box in a nine-stage pipeline. The memorable artifacts are: (1) a decision output that says "Do NOT discount — show the premium bundle, wait 6 hours, then email" with expected uplift, confidence, and reasoning; (2) a three-way experimentation gateway (Accept / Reject / Delay) instead of naive A/B; (3) business-constrained segmentation via MCDA wrappers that reject mathematically pretty but economically useless clusters.
- Naming note: "NextMove" is a working codename, explicitly replaceable without consequence; the subtitle "Behavioral Decision Engine for E-commerce Personalization" is the durable identity.

## Topic: Business problem, stakeholders, and economic frame
- source: PROJECT_VISION.md (§4)
- **Stakeholders and pain:** Marketing/CRM manager — segments in a spreadsheet or CDP UI, blasts campaigns on calendar triggers, discounts by intuition; pain is over-discounting, poor timing, no per-customer reasoning, cannot defend choices to finance. E-commerce product manager — ships UI/merchandising changes via binary A/B; pain is inconclusive tests forced to ship/kill and unresolvable metric conflicts. Growth team — optimizes short-term conversion; pain is reward-hacked metrics with churn and adaptation fatigue invisible until too late. Data scientist — builds propensity models shipped as CSV scores; pain is models never connecting to actions or constraints ("so what?" gap). Merchandising — manages inventory and margin separately from marketing; pain is marketing promoting what merchandising cannot fulfill and discounts eroding margin on scarce stock.
- **Pain points with economic impact:** over-discounting (incentives applied to customers who would have converted anyway transfers margin to no effect); premature experiment decisions; unviable segmentation (context-free clustering produces micro-segments the marketing team cannot operationally serve); trust erosion (intrusive personalization increases short-term engagement and long-term churn simultaneously).
- **Economic frame:** the system's value function is incremental profit under constraints, not conversion: `expected_uplift × margin − incentive_cost − operational_cost`, subject to inventory, campaign, frequency-cap, and trust constraints. Every design choice in the remaining documents flows from this.

## Topic: Non-goals
- source: PROJECT_VISION.md (§5)
- The project explicitly will not attempt: (1) Amazon-scale recommendation infrastructure — no distributed serving, no vector databases at scale, no sub-10ms SLAs; (2) a production CDP replacement — no identity resolution across devices, no consent-management platform, no connector marketplace; (3) a generic, domain-agnostic AI/ML framework; (4) an autonomous marketing agent — no action executed without a human-approvable surface; (5) real-time in-session UI mutation at scale — real-time adaptation is a stretch goal simulated offline and the MVP decides at batch/near-line cadence; (6) live LLM-generated UI (GenUI) in the core path — at most an optional, guarded plugin (pre-generated plus validated content); (7) a front-end product — the engine is headless and the dashboard is a thin demo client; (8) deep-learning maximalism — neural models appear only where the comparison teaches something; (9) real personal data — the project runs on a synthetic-but-realistic e-commerce simulator, no scraping, no PII, no GDPR exposure; (10) multi-tenant SaaS concerns — auth, billing, tenancy out of scope.
